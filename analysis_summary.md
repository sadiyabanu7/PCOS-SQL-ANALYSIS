# Analysis Summary — PCOS Epidemiological Study

## Overview
This analysis examines clinical and demographic predictors of Polycystic 
Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) diagnosis using a dataset of 541 patients from a 
Kerala-based hospital study. Findings are interpreted within a South Asian 
public health context, where PCOS prevalence is disproportionately higher 
than in Western populations.

---

## Finding 1 — Prevalence
**32.6% of patients in this dataset were diagnosed with PCOS** (177 of 541).

This is consistent with published estimates of PCOS prevalence in South 
Asian women (ranging from 22–52%), which are significantly higher than 
the global average of approximately 10%. This disparity has been linked 
to genetic predisposition, dietary patterns, and insulin resistance 
profiles common in South Asian populations.

---

## Finding 2 — Metabolic Markers
| Marker | No PCOS | PCOS |
|--------|---------|------|
| Average Age (yrs) | 32.1 | 30.1 |
| Average BMI | 23.7 | 25.5 |
| Average AMH (ng/mL) | 4.63 | 7.84 |

PCOS patients were on average younger (30.1 vs 32.1 years) and had 
higher BMI (25.5 vs 23.7). Most notably, Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH) 
levels were nearly double in PCOS patients (7.84 vs 4.63 ng/mL).

AMH is increasingly recognised as a reliable biomarker for PCOS diagnosis, 
particularly in South Asian women where ovarian reserve tends to be higher. 
Elevated AMH reflects increased antral follicle count, a hallmark of 
polycystic ovarian morphology.

---

## Finding 3 — Hormonal Profile
| Marker | No PCOS | PCOS |
|--------|---------|------|
| Average FSH (mIU/mL) | 19.19 | 5.17 |
| Average LH (mIU/mL) | 2.61 | 14.4 |
| FSH/LH Ratio | 7.34 | 0.36 |

The data confirms the classic LH/FSH hormonal inversion associated with 
PCOS. In healthy patients, FSH dominates to drive follicular maturation. 
In PCOS patients, LH is dramatically elevated (14.4 vs 2.61 mIU/mL) 
while FSH is suppressed, disrupting normal ovulation.

This hormonal pattern is a standard diagnostic criterion used in clinical 
practice and aligns with the Rotterdam criteria for PCOS diagnosis.

---

## Finding 4 — Symptom Prevalence
| Symptom | No PCOS | PCOS |
|---------|---------|------|
| Weight gain | 22.8% | 68.4% |
| Hirsutism (hair growth) | 12.9% | 57.1% |
| Hair loss | 39.3% | 57.6% |
| Pimples/acne | 27.2% | 71.2% |

Hyperandrogenic symptoms — hirsutism, acne, and hair loss — were 
significantly more prevalent in PCOS patients. Weight gain affected 
68.4% of PCOS patients vs 22.8% without PCOS, highlighting the 
metabolic burden of the condition.

These findings are consistent with the hyperandrogenism criterion of 
the Rotterdam diagnostic criteria and support the need for integrated 
metabolic and endocrine management of PCOS.

---

## Finding 5 — Age Distribution
| Age Group | No PCOS | PCOS |
|-----------|---------|------|
| 18–25 | — | 35 |
| 26–35 | 234 | 117 |
| 36–45 | 95 | 22 |

The highest PCOS burden falls in the **26–35 age group** — the core 
reproductive window. This has significant implications for fertility, 
maternal health outcomes, and antenatal care planning.

Early identification in the 18–25 group (35 cases) is particularly 
important given the long-term metabolic risks associated with untreated 
PCOS, including Type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

---

## Public Health Implications
- Targeted PCOS screening programmes for South Asian women aged 18–35
- Integration of AMH testing into routine gynaecological care
- Lifestyle intervention programmes addressing weight management 
  and insulin resistance
- Enhanced awareness of hyperandrogenic symptoms among primary 
  care providers

---

## Limitations
- Single-centre dataset limits generalisability
- No longitudinal follow-up data available
- Dietary and lifestyle variables not captured
- Ethnicity not disaggregated within South Asian subgroups
